# "The Brain Court", episode 1: *Memory vs. The Deadline*

A vertical short (about 45 s). Inside the head of Aarav, a 23-year-old in his first job, a
courtroom is in session. His manager said a deadline on today's 3 p.m. call, and nobody
in there remembers it. The format can return: a new case every episode (exam syllabus, a
client's number, Mom's grocery list, a friend's birthday plan…).

|---|---|---|---|

Outside the head, briefly: **Aarav** (real-world wrapper), a young Indian guy in a

## Why it should work

- **Hook in the first second:** a gavel and a shouted question, already funny.
- **Relatable for both audiences:** everyone has forgotten what a manager or teacher said.
  The "inside your head" frame works for students and professionals alike.
- **Lots of characters and dialogue:** the last video's weakness was thin dialogue. This one
  has six distinct voices.
- **The product is a character, not an ad:** Srutam is the hero who walks in and saves the
  day, and the proof is concrete (exact time, exact quote).
- **Built to be a series:** "The Brain Court" can return with a new case each week.

## Production plan

1. **Cast sheets** (Canva generate-image): one ensemble lineup of the six brain characters,
   plus Aarav, all in one art style.
2. **Shots** (Canva, using the sheets as references): about 12 vertical illustrations.
3. **Voices** (ElevenLabs): six character voices plus the manager's playback.
4. **Animation**: Remotion layers (camera moves, the push through the forehead, gavel shake,
   falling papers, ringing phones, the waveform glow), plus image-to-video clips for 3–4 key
   moments if a Kling or Runway key is available.
5. **Phone and answer cards, captions, the call to action**: built in code.
